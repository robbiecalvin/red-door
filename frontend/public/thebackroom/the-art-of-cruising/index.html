The Art of Cruising

Why the chase still matters in a digital world

By Badehaus X

Cruising has always involved reading a room. A glance at a bar or a smile on the sidewalk might be an invitation to say hello. For queer people, that language of small gestures has sometimes been a way to find one another in places where being open carried a real cost. The setting changes. The need to pay attention to the person across from you stays.

On a phone, it’s easy to mistake a profile for a whole person. A good first message notices something specific and leaves room for an answer. Ask about the book in their photo or the neighbourhood they love. If the interest is mutual, say what you’re hoping for and ask what they want. A friendly exchange can be enough. A brief reply or silence is a reason to give someone space.

On Badehaus X, Explore lets you see who’s nearby and choose who to talk to. You can start with a conversation and decide what to share as it develops. If you want to meet, suggest a public place you both feel good about. There’s room for a quick hello, a longer night, or a change of plans. That uncertainty is part of what makes a real connection worth having.
